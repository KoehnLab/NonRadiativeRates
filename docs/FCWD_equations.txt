EQUATIONS USED IN nonradiative_rates (branch ic-dev)
====================================================

Franck-Condon weighted density (FCWD), its computation in Fourier space, the analyses built on
it, and the internal-conversion (IC) rate constant.

Units: energies and frequencies in cm^-1 (rcm), hbar = 1 in the vibrational expressions, so that
a phase factor reads exp(-i E t) with E in rcm and t in units of 1/rcm (times 2 pi c). Rate
constants are converted to 1/s at the end (Sec. 5). Low-temperature limit for the quantum-treated
modes: the initial state is the vibrational ground state |0_i> of the initial electronic state.
References in square brackets refer to Sec. 6.


1. MODEL AND NOTATION
=====================

  i, f           initial and final electronic state (IC example: D1 and D0 of TTM-1Cz)
  k, l           normal-mode indices; M: number of vibrational modes
  omega_k        harmonic frequency of mode k
  q_k            dimensionless normal coordinate, q_k = sqrt(omega_k) L_k^T M_at^1/2 (x - x_0)
                 (L_k: orthonormal eigenvector of the mass-weighted Hessian, M_at: atomic masses,
                 x_0: reference geometry; in atomic units)
  d_k            displacement of the final-state minimum relative to the initial geometry, in the
                 dimensionless coordinates of the final state
  S_k = d_k^2/2  Huang-Rhys factor [3]
  v = (v_1, ..., v_M)  vibrational level of the final state, E_v = sum_k omega_k v_k (above the
                 final zero-point energy, ZPE)
  dE             energy at which the FCWD is evaluated (0-0 gap of the model)

Relaxed-gradient model [2]: Hessian and gradient of the final state are computed at the initial
geometry (minimum of the initial state). The final-state surface is harmonic around its
extrapolated minimum,
  d_k = -sqrt(omega_k) (g_mw . L_k) / omega_k^2      (g_mw: mass-weighted final-state gradient),
which lies lambda = sum_k S_k omega_k below the final state at the initial geometry. With the
electronic gap e_vert at the initial geometry, the FCWD is evaluated at dE = e_vert + lambda
(fcwd.relaxed_gradient_gap). Modes with omega_k < 1e-3 rcm (translations, rotations, imaginary
modes) do not enter the FCWD.


2. FCWD
=======

  rho(dE) = sum_v |<v_f|0_i>|^2 delta(dE - E_v)        (golden-rule FCWD, e.g. [4, 11, 12])

The delta function is replaced by a normalized Gaussian window of width sigma centered at dE;
"FCWD" denotes the window average

  rho = sum_E rho(E) w(E),      w(E) = exp(-(E - dE)^2/(2 sigma^2)) / (sigma sqrt(2 pi)) * e_bin

on the energy grid E_m = offset + m e_bin, m = 0 ... nbins-1 (offset = 0 unless low-frequency
modes are treated classically, see below).


3. INDEPENDENT DISPLACED OSCILLATORS
====================================

For identical frequencies in both states and no mode mixing, the vibrational states factorize
and the FCWD is the convolution of single-mode progressions:

  |<v_f|0_i>|^2 = prod_k s_k,v_k^2,       s_v^2 = exp(-S) S^v / v!         (harmonic [3])

(anharmonic: Morse FCFs, Sec. 3.1). Each mode contributes a list of lines (FCF, energy v omega_k),
truncated when the FCFs drop below thrmod or the energy exceeds max_e. Lines are binned: line
energy E -> bin floor(E/e_bin). The FCWD on the grid is the sequential convolution of the binned
progressions ("direct" folding, _fastfold.fold; a binned count of states in the spirit of the
Beyer-Swinehart/Stein-Rabinovitch algorithms [5, 6], with FCF-weighted instead of unit lines [1]):
starting from the initial FCWD rho_0 (delta peak at bin 0, or a Gaussian of width
sqrt(2 lambda_class kT) for classically treated modes), each mode is folded in, and contributions
beyond the last bin are dropped. This is exact for all bins of the grid, since all line energies
are >= 0.

3.1 Morse FCFs
--------------
Modes above anh_thr are described by two identical Morse oscillators (dissociation energy D,
anharmonicity xi = omega/(4 D)), displaced by K = d/sqrt(mred omega) [7, 8]. The overlaps
s_v = <v|0> follow from Eqs. 13 and 16 of [8] (fcf_utils.FcfMorse0.get_I0n): with
a_n = 2 beta - 2n - 1, beta = sqrt(2 mred D)/alpha, Delta = exp(-alpha K), A = (Delta + 1)/2,
B = (a_n + a_0)/2 - 1, C = a_n,

  g_n = <0|n> = N_0 N_n Delta^(a_0/2) I_n(A, B, C)/alpha,
  I_n = Gamma(1+B) Gamma(C+n+1)/(Gamma(n+1) Gamma(C+1)) A^(-1-B) 2F1(1+B, -n; 1+C; 1/A).

Evaluated directly, the Gamma functions (arguments ~ 1/xi) overflow for xi below ~6e-3
(get_I0n_ori), and the polynomial 2F1(., -n; .; 1/A) with 1/A ~ 1 cancels (terms ~ 2^n, result
~ (alpha K/2)^n). get_I0n combines all Gamma functions in logarithms and uses the connection
formula [9] 2F1(a,-n;c;z) = (c-a)_n/(c)_n 2F1(a,-n;a-c-n+1;1-z): with a = 1+B, c = 1+C,
a - c = n - 1 + delta (delta = 0 for identical oscillators) and w = 1 - 1/A = (Delta - 1)/(Delta + 1)
~ -alpha K/2,

  2F1(a, -n; c; 1-w) = (-1)^n/(c)_n sum_(j=0..n) (delta+j)_(n-j) (a)_j (-n)_j/j! w^j,

whose terms do not cancel (j = 0 vanishes at delta = 0 for n >= 1; (j)_(n-j) = (n-1)!/(j-1)!).
For xi -> 0, the FCFs approach the harmonic ones. The line energies are the Morse levels
E_v = omega v - omega xi [(v + 1/2)^2 - 1/4].


4. COMPUTATION IN FOURIER SPACE
===============================

4.1 Discrete Fourier transform
(fast Fourier transform [10]; the time-domain view corresponds to the correlation-function
formulations of rates [11, 12])
------------------------------
Let N (nfft) be a power of two >= 2 nbins (zero padding), and

  t_j = 2 pi j / (N e_bin),   j = 0 ... N/2           (rfft grid, N/2 + 1 points)

The transform of a binned progression h (h_m: FCF in bin m) is

  H(t_j) = sum_m h_m exp(-2 pi i m j / N) = sum_lines FCF exp(-i E_line t_j)  (E_line binned),

i.e. the (discrete) generating function sum_v FCF_v exp(-i E_v t) of the mode. Convolution in
energy is multiplication in Fourier space:

  rho_m = IDFT[ R_0(t) prod_k H_k(t) ]_m,      R_0 = DFT of rho_0.

- Wrap-around: the DFT convolution is circular. Lines of a single mode beyond the grid are dropped
  (they cannot contribute to bins < nbins), so each H_k has support < nbins; with N >= 2 nbins,
  contributions of combinations whose total energy exceeds N e_bin wrap around into low bins.
  Their weight is negligible for the cases studied (checked against direct folding to 1e-15 for
  FCWDs ~1e-6); it is not exactly zero in general.
- Exact phase factors: H_k(t_j) is evaluated directly from the lines with a table of
  exp(-2 pi i r/N), r = (m j) mod N (no sine/cosine calls, no FFT of mostly empty arrays), in blocks
  of j (memory independent of the grid apart from the table).
- Binning: line energies are rounded down to the grid, i.e. combinations of many lines are shifted
  by up to (number of excited modes) x e_bin. Relative binning error of rho at e_bin = 1 rcm
  (against an unbinned evaluation, test case with ~100 modes): ~1e-4; proportional
  to e_bin.
- Round-off: Fourier-space round-off is absolute (~1e-17 per bin for the normalized FCWD, ~1e-19
  for window averages), whereas direct folding of positive numbers is accurate relative to each
  value. Relative errors therefore grow for FCWDs far in the tail (observed: 1e-6 ... 1e-4 for
  FCWDs ~1e-14 ... 1e-16).

4.2 Window averages without inverse transform (Parseval)
--------------------------------------------------------
For real sequences a, b of length N (zero padded),

  sum_m a_m b_m = (1/N) sum_(j=0)^(N-1) A_j conj(B_j)
               = (1/N) [ A_0 B_0* + 2 Re sum_(j=1)^(N/2-1) A_j B_j* + A_(N/2) B_(N/2)* ]

(one-sided weights c_j = 1, 2, ..., 2, 1 on the rfft grid). Hence

  rho = (1/N) Re sum_j c_j R_0(t_j) conj(W(t_j)) prod_k H_k(t_j) = (1/N) Re sum_j base(t_j) prod_k H_k(t_j),

with base = c R_0 conj(W) (W: DFT of the window). All Fourier-space quantities below are such
inner products with base; no inverse transform is needed.

4.3 Analyses (single call each)
-------------------------------
Mode-resolved analysis of the FCWD as introduced in [1] (omission of single modes, combination
effects), here evaluated in a single pass in Fourier space.
Prefix and suffix products pre_k = base H_0 ... H_(k-1), suf_k = H_(k+1) ... H_(M-1) give, without
division by H_k (which may become small):

  FCWD without mode k        rho(-k)  = (1/N) Re sum_j pre_k suf_k
  FCWD without modes k < l   rho(-k,-l) = (1/N) Re sum_j pre_k H_(k+1) ... H_(l-1) suf_l
                             (for fixed k, a running product is extended over l)

"Omitting" a mode means treating it as undisplaced (H_k = 1). Output: remaining FCWD in % of the
full FCWD, rho(-k)/rho and rho(-k,-l)/rho, and the synergy factor

  Q_kl = rho rho(-k,-l) / (rho(-k) rho(-l))      (> 1: reinforcing, < 1: replacing modes).

Minimal mode set: greedy backward elimination (a standard sequential selection scheme, cf. [14];
omit the mode whose omission retains the largest
FCWD, as long as >= fraction of rho is retained), each step one prefix/suffix pass over the
remaining modes. Dominant final states: exact k-best fold in energy space with back pointers (dynamic programming
keeping the k best partial states per bin, analogous to list Viterbi decoding [13]);
an initial distribution rho_0 is folded into the window, w_eff(E) = sum_b rho_0(b) w(E + b).
Round-off estimates: eps x (number of modes) x sum_j |term_j| / N.



4.4 Benefits and limitations of the Fourier-space formulation
--------------------------------------------------------------
For the FCWD alone, folding in Fourier space is only slightly faster than direct folding (e.g.
0.42 s vs. 0.54 s for the IC example). The main benefits arise from the structure: every quantity
is a product of per-mode factors, evaluated as one inner product with the window (Sec. 4.2).

1. Analyses in a single pass: omission of single modes and of pairs (Sec. 4.3) and the minimal
   mode set are different products of the same mode transforms. Speed-ups: single-mode analysis
   x30-60 (IC example x64), pair analyses feasible (all ~M^2/2 pairs in a few seconds).
2. No inverse transform: window averages follow directly from Parseval's theorem. A change of the
   window only changes the base factor, not the product of the modes, e.g. for energy scans rho(E)
   without recomputing the mode transforms.
3. Interchangeable factors: each factor of the product can be replaced independently, e.g. a
   classical distribution of low-frequency modes by its (Gaussian) transform. Further extensions
   would be multiplicative factors as well: finite temperature (thermal generating function per
   mode, hot bands), line broadening (Lorentzian <-> exp(-gamma |t|)), or analytic generating
   functions of groups of modes (e.g. with mode mixing), which are exact without truncation of
   the FCF lists and without binning.
4. Extension to time-correlation-function methods [11, 12]: finite temperature or (numerically
   transformed) anharmonic factors fit into the same formalism.
5. Memory: the block-wise evaluation with a table of exact phase factors keeps the memory demand
   independent of the grid (apart from the table).

Limitations:
- Round-off is absolute (Sec. 4.1): the relative accuracy decreases far in the tail
  (FCWD <~ 1e-15); there, direct folding of positive numbers remains more accurate, which is why
  both methods are kept (fcwd_method = "direct" | "fft").
- Wrap-around of the circular convolution requires zero padding (N >= 2 nbins).
- Modes given as FCF lists remain binned (binning error ~1e-4 for rho at e_bin = 1 rcm,
  proportional to e_bin).


5. IC RATE CONSTANT
===================

  k_IC = pi/hbar |NAC|^2 rho                                                         [1]

with the norm of the derivative-coupling (NAC) vector projected onto the normal modes of the
final state (ic.effective_nac: mass weighting, projection, frequency weighting; modes below
1 rcm do not contribute). With |NAC| in rcm and rho in 1/rcm: k [1/s] = (pi/hbar) (100 h c)
|NAC|^2 rho, h, hbar, c in SI (ic.non_radiative).
